WORKBENCH REFERENCE

Orientation comes first.

This is a view of the add-in card edge, with the contacts pointing down toward the motherboard slot. It is not a top view of a motherboard socket.

Front = component side B: bracket / I/O on the left, B1 nearest the bracket. Back = solder side A: turn the PCB around its vertical axis; bracket on the right, A1 nearest the bracket. The key is between contacts 11 and 12 on both sides. Component placement on modern cards is not a reliable orientation cue.

Hover or focus a contact for a tooltip and detail preview. Click, tap or press Enter to lock it; click again to release it. Related pins automatically open the correct side. Search and filters cover both sides; use the results list to jump to a hidden-side contact. The lane picker isolates all four contacts of a lane.

Both A/B shows component B above and mirrored solder A below. Each row is a separate physical PCB view. Related-pin selection keeps both rows visible. On mobile, the Contact close-up magnifies the locked contact and its neighbors; tap a neighbor or use Previous / Next in visual order. Locate scrolls the full connector to your selection. Hover previews do not move the close-up.

Basic mode shows the pin, signal and function. Repair mode adds electrical information, measurements, warnings and related contacts. All directions explicitly name the motherboard and add-in card.

Revision scope: choose the documented CEM 1.1 map or the CEM 4.0 v0.9 draft map, which assigns B12 to CLKREQ# and B30 to PWRBRK#. This is not a claim of certification against the current PCI-SIG revision. Optional functions and board routing must be checked against your hardware schematic.

Keyboard shortcuts

← / → move in visual order · Enter / Space select a focused pin · Esc clear · F front · B back · D both A/B · / search · ? help. Shortcuts are inactive while typing.

Measurement safety

Disconnect mains, auxiliary GPU power and any batteries before resistance measurements; wait for capacitors to discharge. Standby power can remain present when a PC is soft-off. Use insulated probes and avoid shorting adjacent fingers. PCIe data and reference clock pairs require suitable high-bandwidth differential equipment; a multimeter cannot validate signal quality. No universal GPU resistance thresholds are provided.
